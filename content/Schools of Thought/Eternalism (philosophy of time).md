**Overview**
Eternalism is a metaphysical theory of time which holds that past, present, and future events are equally real. Time is viewed as a tenseless dimension (often compared to space) in which all moments exist ontologically; temporal differences are relations among events rather than differences in existence. This view contrasts with presentism and with some versions of the growing block theory in its treatment of the future.

**Development Period**
Ancient precursors appear in some classical and Hellenistic thought, but the modern articulation grew from 19th–20th century physics and metaphysics, especially following developments in relativity theory (early 20th century) and analytic philosophy of time (mid 20th century onward).

**Key Figures**
- J.M.E. McTaggart (formulated influential arguments about the unreality of time)
- Henri Bergson (critic of certain tenseless accounts; important interlocutor)
- Hermann Minkowski (spacetime framework influential for eternalist interpretations)
- D.H. Mellor (analytic defender of the tenseless theory)
- David Lewis (famously defended a modal/temporal realism with perdurantist commitments)
