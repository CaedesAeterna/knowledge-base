**Overview**  
Presentism is the metaphysical view that only present entities and events exist. The past no longer exists and the future does not yet exist; reality is confined to a fleeting present moment. Temporal talk is often analyzed in tensed terms, and the ontology of presentism contrasts sharply with tenseless accounts like eternalism.

**Development Period**  
Presentist ideas trace back to ancient thought but were articulated more precisely in modern metaphysics and analytic philosophy from the 19th century onward, with renewed attention in the late 20th and early 21st centuries due to debates over tense, relativity, and the ontology of time.

Key Figures  
- Augustine of Hippo — Early theological and philosophical reflections on time and the present (notably in Confessions) influenced later debates about whether only the present is real and how temporal experience relates to metaphysics.
- Arthur Prior — 20th-century logician and philosopher who developed tense logic (temporal logic) and argued that tensed language tracks objective tense; his work provided formal tools and philosophical arguments in support of presentism.
- A. N. Prior — (same person as Arthur Prior; often cited as A. N. Prior in publications) Made foundational contributions to temporal logic and defended the metaphysical significance of tense.
- John Lucas — Contemporary philosopher who has argued for a robust conception of temporal becoming and defended presentist intuitions against tenseless alternatives, often engaging with issues about consciousness and the passage of time.
- Richard Gale — Metaphysician who has developed arguments for presentism and critiqued tenseless accounts; he has worked on the logic and ontology supporting a present-centered metaphysics.
